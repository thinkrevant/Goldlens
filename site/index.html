The problem

MCX sells the same gold four ways.

GOLDM in 100 g lots, GOLDTEN in 10 g, GOLDGUINEA in 8 g, GOLDPETAL in 1 g. Quoted per 10 g, per 8 g, per 1 g, at 995 or 999 purity. On screen they look like four unrelated markets.

GoldLens puts them on one scale.

Every price becomes rupees per 10 g of 999 gold: price × (10 ÷ quoted grams) × (999 ÷ purity). Watch four lines become one.

The gaps that remain are the signal.

A few basis points of difference are left. GoldLens asks one question about each: is this gap unusual for this pair, and would closing it pay after costs?

How it works

From four messy prices to one decision, every day.

01

Ingest & guard

MCX's daily Bhavcopy, with date checks, holiday detection and stale-price quarantine. Every contract tracked by its own expiry.

02

Normalise & fit the curve

One scale, then a daily fair curve: spot, carry, and each contract's premium, so different expiries compare fairly.

03

Scan, quietly

An alert needs an unusual gap, liquid legs, and an expected reversion worth 1.5× round-trip costs. Most days: silence.

04

Explain in plain words

An AI analyst writes the brief in English or Hindi, using only numbers GoldLens computed. It explains; it never calculates.

    Found in the data

    The small contracts went rich in the 2026 gold rush.

    Monthly premium of GOLDGUINEA and GOLDPETAL over GOLDM, after units, purity and carry are removed. Retail demand pushed it past +300 bps. And the premium never fully closes, even at expiry, which is why GoldLens scores every gap against its own history instead of assuming it's free money.
